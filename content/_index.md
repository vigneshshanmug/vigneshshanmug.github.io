Vignesh Shanmugam is a hematopathologist and physician-scientist at Brigham and Women's Hospital and the Broad Institute of MIT and Harvard, and an Instructor in Pathology at Harvard Medical School. He leads a research program focused on how lymphoma cells and their precursors interact with, and depend on, the immune and stromal cells of their microenvironment.

His research applies genome-scale spatial transcriptomics, single-cell genomics and computational methods to human lymphoma tissue. With colleagues at the Broad Institute and Brigham and Women's Hospital, he has mapped the pro-tumorigenic cellular niche of classic Hodgkin lymphoma at genome scale and identified tumor cell survival factors, contributed to spatial genomics methods such as Slide-tags, and studied the stromal and macrophage remodeling that accompanies follicular lymphoma transformation. His earlier work in diagnostic and molecular hematopathology includes studies of Notch activation in B-cell lymphomas, RAS–MAPK pathway mutations in histiocytic neoplasms, and next-generation sequencing of peripheral blood in patients with cytopenias.

He received his M.D. with Academic Distinction and Honors in Research from Weill Cornell Medical College in Qatar in 2016. He trained in anatomic pathology at Brigham and Women's Hospital, where he was Chief Resident, and completed the Geraldine S. Pinkus Fellowship in Hematopathology before joining the Broad Institute. His research has been supported by the Damon Runyon Cancer Research Foundation and the Lymphoma Research Foundation, and he received the American Society for Clinical Investigation Emerging Generation Award in 2025.
